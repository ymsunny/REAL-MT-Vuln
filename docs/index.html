01 / THE QUESTION

Helpful knowledge.
Misplaced trust.

Retrieval improves translation only when the model knows what to trust.

Correct context helps models translate idioms. But what happens when the retrieved meaning is wrong?

REAL-MT stands for REtrieval-Augmented LLM-based Machine Translation. We stress-test its reliability with controlled noisy meanings across high-, medium-, and low-resource translation settings.

The study connects three levels of evidence: what changes in the translation, how the model uses the hint, and what robustness interventions give up.

Read the abstract +

REtrieval-Augmented LLM-based Machine Translation (REAL-MT) shows promise for knowledge-intensive tasks like idiomatic translation, but its reliability under noisy retrieval, a common challenge in real-world deployment, remains poorly understood. To address this gap, we propose a noise synthesis framework and new metrics to systematically evaluate REAL-MT’s reliability across high-, medium-, and low-resource language pairs. Using both open- and closed-sourced models, including standard LLMs and large reasoning models (LRMs), we find that models heavily rely on retrieved context, and this dependence is significantly more detrimental in low-resource language pairs, producing nonsensical translations. Although LRMs possess enhanced reasoning capabilities, they show no improvement in error correction and are even more susceptible to noise, tending to rationalize incorrect contexts. Attention analysis reveals a shift from the source idiom to noisy content, while confidence increases despite declining accuracy, indicating poor self-monitoring. To mitigate these issues, we investigate training-free and fine-tuning strategies, which improve robustness at the cost of performance in clean contexts, revealing a fundamental trade-off. Our findings highlight the limitations of current approaches, underscoring the need for self-verifying integration mechanisms.

04 / LOOKING INSIDE THE FAILURE

The hint gets attention.
The error gets confidence.

Attention and output entropy offer two views of context dependence. Together, they motivate better self-verification.

ATTENTION ALLOCATION

Focus shifts toward the hint.

Across the analyzed conditions, models attend to contextual meaning even when it contradicts the source idiom.

CONFIDENCE & FIDELITY

More certain. Less accurate.

With opposite-meaning hints, idiom-span entropy falls while Fidelity declines in Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Lower entropy means higher confidence.

These are diagnostics of the evaluated systems. Attention allocation alone does not establish a causal explanation of model behavior.

Study scope and interpretation +

Controlled stress tests. The study synthesizes noisy idiom meanings to isolate retrieval failure modes. These conditions do not estimate how frequently each failure occurs in deployed retrieval systems.

Measured scores. Fidelity and CAR use automatic judgments with human validation; both remain imperfect measurements. All interactive numeric results on this page come from the supplied AAAI conference paper, Tables 3 and 4.

Scope of the evidence. Reasoning-mode results concern the evaluated Qwen3-8B configurations. Attention and confidence analyses are diagnostic, and mitigation trade-offs depend on the model, language direction and intervention.
